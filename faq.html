Section 02

Tyre Care & Technical Support

It is recommended to have your wheel alignment checked every 10,000 to 12,000 miles, or at least once a year. You should also get it checked if you hit a large pothole, curb, or notice uneven tyre wear.

Alignment adjusts the angles of the wheels so they are parallel to each other and perpendicular to the ground. Balancing involves placing small weights on the rim to ensure the tyre spins without causing vibrations.

Look for the tread wear indicators (small bars inside the grooves). If the tread is flush with these bars, you have reached the legal limit of 1.6mm. We highly recommend replacing them when they reach 3mm for safety in wet conditions.

Nitrogen molecules are larger than oxygen, meaning they escape through the tyre walls at a slower rate. This helps maintain correct tyre pressure longer, which improves fuel economy and tyre lifespan.

Generally, manufacturers do not recommend repairing run-flat tyres because it is difficult to see structural damage that may have occurred while driving on it flat. We assess these on a strict case-by-case basis but mostly recommend replacement.

The correct pressure is usually located on a placard inside the driver's side door jam, inside the fuel filler cap, or in the vehicle's owner manual. Never use the "Max Pressure" written on the tyre sidewall as your daily driving pressure.
